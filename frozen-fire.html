Environmental Sciences Undergraduate Seminar

Frozen Fire: Methane, Permafrost, and Gas Hydrates in a Rapidly Changing Arctic.

This interdisciplinary seminar connects the physical behavior of frozen sediments with gas-hydrate stability, methane observations across the Arctic, and related environmental and engineering questions.

I draw on my earlier laboratory research and more recent observations of Arctic landscapes to help students move between material-scale mechanisms and larger environmental processes.

Seminar themes ↗My teaching approach ↗

THE SCIENCE

One material system.
Many processes.

The talk introduces concepts and studies them at different scales, from hydrate-bearing pore space to observations across Arctic landscapes and shelves.

01 / FROZEN-GROUND MATERIALS

More than ice

Frozen sediments can contain mineral grains, pore ice, unfrozen water, gases, and gas hydrates. Understanding that mix makes Arctic ground behavior less mysterious.

Slides 28–34, 49
02 / PRESSURE & TEMPERATURE

When hydrates are stable

Methane hydrates are crystalline compounds whose stability depends on physical conditions. Changes in porewater chemistry and temperature help students connect equations to phase changes.

Slides 32–34, 53–55
03 / OBSERVATIONAL EVIDENCE

Where does methane come from?

Arctic gas seeps, acoustic observations, and gas-emission craters provide examples for evaluating competing explanations. Methane observations do not, on their own, prove that gas hydrates caused the emissions.

Slides 8–27
04 / APPLICATIONS

Science beyond the laboratory

Students explore hydrate-related drilling hazards, gas transport, potential resources, and CO₂-hydrate concepts, distinguishing proposed technologies from demonstrated outcomes.

Slides 35–40, 67–69

TEACHING THROUGH EVIDENCE

From observation
to explanation.

I use scientific examples to show that evidence for a process at one scale does not automatically establish its importance at another.

Ask what is measured

Distinguish observed methane release from inferences about the gas source.

Compare physical mechanisms

Relate pressure, temperature, porewater chemistry, and sediment structure to phase behavior.

Connect science to decisions

Consider what data would be needed to evaluate drilling risks or environmental impacts.
