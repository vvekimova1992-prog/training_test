RESEARCH / ARCTIC PERMAFROST

Why thaw differs from one patch of tundra to the next.

Understanding permafrost change requires connecting snow and water at the surface with ice, sediment, heat, and chemical processes below ground. My work brings laboratory experiments, geophysics, field observations, and remote sensing together to explain those connections.

THE SCIENTIFIC QUESTION

Small distances.
Different outcomes.

Even neighboring sites can respond differently to similar climate conditions. Snowdrifts, standing water, vegetation, ground ice, and engineered surfaces shift the energy and water balances that determine when and where thaw occurs.

In Utqiaġvik, we observed shallow thaw beneath persistent snowdrifts but thaw two to three times deeper in adjacent ponded ground.

This contrast motivates my research: which combinations of surface and subsurface properties create such different thaw responses, and how can we measure and predict them?

CURRENT RESEARCH

Field evidence meets physical mechanisms.

My investigations in coastal Alaska build on more than a decade of experimental research on frozen sediments, heat and mass transfer, and phase transitions.

01 / LANDSCAPE PROCESSES

Snow, hydrology & thaw

At Utqiaġvik sites, I study snow persistence, drainage, near-surface temperature, water quality, vegetation, and infrastructure-related thaw variability. I combine in situ measurements with satellite observations to examine changing conditions over space and time.

02 / SUBSURFACE GEOPHYSICS

Seeing below the tundra

Electrical resistivity tomography (ERT) and ground-penetrating radar (GPR) help describe near-surface frozen-ground conditions across natural and infrastructure-modified terrain. Our published geophysics study was a six-author collaboration supported by research-site access, local logistical support, and CRREL expertise. These methods connect surface observations to subsurface conditions.

03 / EXPERIMENTAL GEOCRYOLOGY

Salt, ice & methane hydrates

Working with Evgeny Chuvilin and laboratory colleagues, I investigated how temperature, pressure, salinity, and sediment properties govern salt migration. In frozen hydrate-bearing sediments, salt transfer can induce pore-ice melting and methane hydrate dissociation even below 0°C.

Field measurements

Thaw depth, snow, temperature, microtopography, and water quality.

Geophysics & Earth observations

ERT, GPR, Sentinel-2 snow metrics, and spatial comparisons.

Controlled experiments

Heat and mass transfer, salinity, ice formation, and frozen-sediment properties.

RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

From measurements to better predictions.

My independent research program will test which mechanisms, ground properties, and environmental observations are needed to predict local thaw and support Arctic planning. The objectives below are future research directions, not completed projects.

OBJECTIVE 01

Connect surface and subsurface controls

Measure how snow redistribution, drainage, soil moisture, vegetation, ground ice, and infrastructure interact, and determine which parameters can be mapped with satellite imagery and which require local sampling.

OBJECTIVE 02

Test freezing and thawing mechanisms

Use controlled experiments on sediments with varied salinity, water and ice content, and composition to measure thermal properties, phase transitions, salt movement, and thaw-front development. Work with geomechanics collaborators on settlement and deformation.

OBJECTIVE 03

Evaluate predictive skill and risks

Test models against independent sites and seasons, assess the value of measured versus generalized parameters, and develop updatable maps of permafrost degradation susceptibility and infrastructure risk with community and engineering partners.

Geophysics of built and natural terrain

Our peer-reviewed study integrates ERT, GPR, and thaw probing across residential, gravel-road, snow-fence, and tundra sites. The published paper records coauthor roles, local research access, UIC Science logistics, and CRREL survey support.

ONGOING / MANUSCRIPT IN REVIEW

Snow persistence and land use

I investigate snow redistribution, melt timing, albedo, and built-environment differences using Sentinel-2 imagery, temperature records, and field observations. Detailed unpublished maps and quantitative findings are not shown here.

ONGOING INVESTIGATION

Arctic lake and lagoon water quality

Field measurements at Isatkoak Lagoon, Middle Salt Lagoon, and Imikpuk Lake use a YSI EXO sonde to document temperature, conductivity, oxygen, pH, turbidity, chlorophyll, and fluorescent dissolved organic matter. Analyses and interpretations remain ongoing.
