# Valentina Ekimova — Arctic permafrost research

Personal academic website highlighting research in Arctic permafrost, snow, hydrology, near-surface geophysics, frozen sediments, and climate-related landscape change. The portfolio includes field photographs from Utqiaġvik, Alaska.

## Pages and files

- `index.html` — research profile, fieldwork photographs, selected publications, teaching, outreach, and contact.
- `cv.html` — concise academic CV.
- `styles.css` and `script.js` — responsive styling, navigation, publication filtering, and photo gallery.
- `assets/` — web-optimized field photography.

## Photography and research content

Photographs illustrate winter snow observations, water-quality fieldwork, tundra surface water, permafrost landforms and exposures, Arctic infrastructure, field instruction, and community engagement. Web files omit camera EXIF metadata. Confirm publication rights and, where applicable, permissions of recognizable participants before distributing the website publicly.

## Gas-hydrate research

[Gas Hydrates & Frozen Sediments](hydrates.html) documents laboratory experimental geocryology, salt migration, pore-ice melting, hydrate dissociation, and published work from 2019–2026. The page connects the Skoltech PhD and collaborative research history with the contemporary Arctic field program. Scientific findings link to primary journal or publisher sources, including Russian-language publications in *Криосфера Земли*. It credits Evgeny Chuvilin and research collaborators, distinguishes laboratory evidence from field-scale methane emissions, and contains an original conceptual schematic rather than reproducing third-party scientific figures.

## Undergraduate seminar

[Frozen Fire](frozen-fire.html) presents the topics of the Environmental Sciences undergraduate seminar *Frozen Fire: Methane, Permafrost, and Gas Hydrates in a Rapidly Changing Arctic*. It connects teaching with experimental geocryology and Arctic field research. The page is a written overview, not a reproduction of the 73-page source slide deck. It displays the burning-hydrate demonstration photograph by Dinara Davletshina with an explicit credit, while attributing the associated video to Ekaterina Krivokhat without hosting the video. Other third-party scientific figures and unpublished snow-analysis plots are not reproduced. It uses existing fieldwork photography from the website.
