UTQIAĠVIK / FIELD SCIENCE / PEOPLE

Research built on relationships.

Arctic field research depends on the knowledge, participation, and practical support of many people. My work is part of a broader interdisciplinary effort and ongoing exchanges with local organizations, students, and colleagues.

COMMUNITY-INFORMED RESEARCH

Listening, measuring,
sharing.

Research questions arise from scientific evidence and conversations about locally observed change, water, and infrastructure. My research activities and contributions from local organizations are described separately, rather than treating every exchange as formal co-design.

LOCAL QUESTIONS

Understanding changing water

Conversations with organizations in Utqiaġvik helped motivate my lake and lagoon investigations, including a drinking-water source. These exchanges identify observations, questions, and monitoring needs that merit further study.

FIELD MEASUREMENTS

Water quality in three water bodies

I initiated lagoon research with seed-funding support. Field measurements at Isatkoak Lagoon, Middle Salt Lagoon, and Imikpuk Lake use a YSI EXO multiparameter sonde for temperature, conductivity, dissolved oxygen, pH, turbidity, chlorophyll, and fluorescent dissolved organic matter. Analyses remain ongoing.

STUDENT PARTICIPATION

Learning through research

My teaching and research with Iḷisaġvik College students includes field measurements, lagoon-data analysis, and communication of observations. Their work contributes to research as well as learning.

Sharing findings and listening: In May 2026, I presented Isatkoak Lagoon observations at Barrow Utilities and Electric Cooperative (BUECI). Our subsequent conversations with the water-treatment team include monitoring priorities and questions about lagoon conditions. Such exchanges do not imply that BUECI endorses particular scientific conclusions or management recommendations.

LAND & COMMUNITY

My research in Utqiaġvik takes place on ancestral Iñupiat lands. I acknowledge the Iñupiat communities who have cared for these lands, ice, and waters across generations and continue to steward them today. I seek to recognize contributions accurately and respect expectations about site access, knowledge sharing, and the use of research findings.
